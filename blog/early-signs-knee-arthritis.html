Early Signs of Knee Arthritis

Patient education from Dr. Chethan Kumar’s orthopaedic practice in JP Nagar and RR Nagar, Bengaluru.

Knee osteoarthritis often starts quietly. Patients from JP Nagar and Jayanagar notice stiffness after sitting, a dull ache after walking in the park, or swelling by evening. These are early signs — not a reason to panic, and not an automatic ticket to knee replacement.

Morning stiffness that eases within 30 minutes is typical of osteoarthritis. Inflammatory arthritis may feel different: longer stiffness, multiple joints, or systemic symptoms. That distinction matters for treatment.

Other clues include difficulty squatting for floor activities, discomfort on stairs (especially descending), and a feeling that the knee is “catching.” Crepitus (grinding) is common and not always serious on its own.

Risk factors include age, prior sports injury, excess body weight, and family history. If you had an old meniscus or ligament injury, arthritis can appear earlier.

What to do first: keep moving with low-impact activity such as walking or cycling as tolerated, strengthen quadriceps and hips, and avoid crash diets that lose muscle. Over-the-counter pain relief should be used as advised by a doctor if you have other illnesses.

See an orthopaedic specialist in RR Nagar or JP Nagar if pain wakes you at night, the knee gives way, or walking distance is shrinking despite six weeks of sensible self-care. X-rays taken standing show joint space better than lying films.

Early arthritis is usually managed without surgery: physiotherapy, activity modification, sometimes injections. Replacement is discussed when quality of life is persistently poor and imaging shows advanced wear.

This article is educational and does not replace an examination. Individual advice depends on your examination and X-rays.
