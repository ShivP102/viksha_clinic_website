Sports Injury Prevention

Patient education from Dr. Chethan Kumar’s orthopedic practice in JP Nagar and RR Nagar, Bengaluru.

Most sports injuries are not freak accidents. Load that rises faster than tissue can adapt — a new running plan, tournament cricket every weekend, or a sudden gym PR — is a common story in Bengaluru South clinics.

Prevention pillars: progressive training, strength (especially hips, calves and rotator cuff for overhead sports), adequate sleep, and respecting pain that alters your movement. Warm-up should raise heart rate and rehearse the sport, not only static stretching.

Football and cricket players should include change-of-direction drills and landing mechanics to reduce ACL risk. Runners need calf and foot capacity and sensible weekly mileage increases.

Equipment matters: worn shoes, poorly fitted bats, or wet outfields. Junior athletes in Jayanagar academies often play one sport year-round; varied movement in the off-season helps.

If you feel a pop, swelling within hours, or the joint gives way, stop. Early assessment by a sports injury doctor in JP Nagar can distinguish a sprain from a ligament tear.

Return-to-play should be based on strength, hop tests and confidence — not the calendar alone. Re-injury is more expensive than an extra two weeks of rehab.
